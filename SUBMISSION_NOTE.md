# FollowUp — submission note

I built a lead follow-up desk for a packaging-equipment sales team using Python, Streamlit, and SQLite. It includes 25 fictional leads assigned to five reps and 25 WhatsApp-style conversations. Reps see overdue and today's follow-ups first, open a lead's conversation and recommendation, edit and save a draft, and record it as sent. Adding a lead or chat triggers an updated recommendation. Clickable summary cards filter due, overdue, Hot, or active leads; temperature labels use red, yellow, and blue for clarity.

The Gemini agent reads the lead and conversation and returns a validated Hot/Warm/Cold tag, short reason, follow-up date, and WhatsApp-style draft. It considers buying intent, requested timing, and the latest buyer message; it is instructed not to invent prices, specifications, or prior actions. Explicit refusals pause outreach. A transparent local policy provides a working baseline and visible fallback when the model is unavailable. Recording a sent message logs the exact edited text and schedules the next check-in; the app never sends WhatsApp messages.

I used OpenAI Codex to assist with implementation, sample data, and tests. Live Gemini inference has been verified directly and through the running app. Twenty-three automated tests cover persistence, requested dates, opt-outs, stale submissions, provider selection, structured responses, fallback, and the rep workflow/card filters. SQLite persists locally, but local files on free Streamlit Community Cloud can reset; durable hosted storage would be my next infrastructure improvement. Next I would also evaluate recommendations against rep-labelled conversations and improve multilingual intent and scheduling. Login, actual WhatsApp sending, and payments were kept out of scope.
